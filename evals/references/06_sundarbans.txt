The Sundarbans is the world's largest mangrove forest, spread across 10,277 sq km of the Ganges Delta where the Ganges, Brahmaputra and Meghna meet the Bay of Bengal, shared between West Bengal and Bangladesh. It holds four UNESCO World Heritage protected areas and supports 453 recorded fauna species. Cyclone Sidr damaged around 40% of the forest in 2007, and rising salinity from sea-level rise and reduced freshwater flow is the longer-term threat, alongside the Rampal coal plant.
